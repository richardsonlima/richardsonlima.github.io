> There are works that do not exist to be heard, but to be inhabited. In Guillaume Poncelet's work, I found something that is not just neo-classical music: it is a **phenomenological mirror**.

For many, the minimalism of a solo piano might sound "empty" or "monotonous." For minds oriented toward internal coherence and high-density sensory processing, it is where reality finally makes sense. This essay explores the relationship between Poncelet's music and a cognitive architecture that seeks not entertainment, but sensory truth without mediation.

---

## I. [Iridescence](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=WPGawUi0WXQ&list=RDWPGawUi0WXQ&index=1&t=208s): The Density that Demands Presence

When listening to *Iridescence*, the sensation is one of **gravity**. This music works with slow layers, suspended harmony, and progressions that do not "resolve" quickly; this often accesses deep internal contents, especially states of contemplation and implicit emotional memory.

From my perspective, such music tends to strongly activate the limbic system (emotions) with little initial rational mediation. It is as if the music "skips" the analytical cortex and speaks directly to regions where non-verbalized experiences reside: sense and silence. It has no relation to sadness; it is density. Something that demands presence, not escape.

I also do not perceive a link to emotional fragility. On the contrary: it usually mediates a high level of introspection, symbolic imagination, and openness to experience. Discomfort may arise because the music does not distract; it invites. And we are not always ready for what appears when we accept that invitation.

A useful metaphor: this music works like a deep, dark lake. Those who look from afar see only the calm surface. Those who enter feel the cold, the pressure, and the vastness. It is not dangerous, but it requires breath and emotional maturity.

When something artistic touches you this way, it is not the music that is “heavy;” it is because it found something alive inside you that usually remains silent. It is a manifestation of elevated sensory aesthetic sensitivity, deep emotional processing, and the relationship between minimalist music and contemplative states, such as introspective *flow* versus rumination.

## II. [L’autre soi](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=WPGawUi0WXQ&list=RDWPGawUi0WXQ&index=1&t=472s): The Call to Self-Observation

*L’autre soi* delivers an even deeper experience than *Iridescence*. The title itself provides the key: “the other self.” This work does not just evoke emotion; it summons identity.

If in *Iridescence* there is contemplation and mature emotional density, in *L’autre soi* the movement is more introspective and disorganizing. Musically, Poncelet uses hypnotic repetition, small variations, and long silences that create a very specific effect: the listener begins to lose the sense of “who observes” and “who feels.” It is almost an internal mirroring; this usually activates states close to what can be called **deep self-observation**, not intellectual, but sensory.

This type of music reduces external anchoring and favors the emergence of contents from the silent narrative self: parts of you that exist before words, before explanations. Therefore, it may sound more “abyssal” than *Iridescence*. It is not just emotion; it is recognition.

In people with a high degree of internal complexity, this music usually touches three layers at the same time:

- The self that feels;
- The self that observes;
- And the self that has not yet been integrated.

Depth can even be uncomfortable because *L’autre soi* does not offer catharsis (cleansing). It does not lead to a climax. It remains. And **remaining with oneself, without distraction, is one of the most demanding experiences that exist**.

Think: when something seems “too deep,” often it is not excess; it is a lack of escape. The relationship lies with the concept of the parallel self and how art accesses non-integrated parts of identity. *L'autre soi* offers not catharsis, but **permanence**.

## III. [Yaki Imo](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=WPGawUi0WXQ&list=RDWPGawUi0WXQ&index=1&t=785s): Structural Affinity of Mind

This is not a matter of common musical taste; it is structural cognitive affinity. Many people seek stimuli to regulate emotions; others seek stimuli to explore internal states. If you belong to the second group, works like *Yaki Imo* do not "pull you down;" they open up space. Where many feel emptiness or boredom, some can perceive a sensory field, psychic texture, and depth.

There are clear axes in these works. First, the relationship with silence and with sensory (non-relational) ambiguity. *Most people need voice, lyrics, and explicit narrative* because silence can activate anxiety. Contemplative music removes cognitive crutches. Those who do not easily tolerate sensory ambiguity may find this type of work "depressing" or "boring." Those who tolerate, or rather, inhabit sensory ambiguity, find expansion there.

Some people have an emotional system matured enough not to avoid intense states. However, many fear non-nameable emotions because they can confuse intensity with disorganization. Yet, some individuals can feel something deep without needing to immediately explain, control, or escape. This is rare; it is a form of silent emotional courage.

There are minds that operate more by depth than by stimulus. They are people who do not seek quick dopamine, obvious catharsis, or entertainment. They seek internal coherence, resonance, and implicit meaning. These works function as high-resolution mirrors: they do not show “stories;” they show states of being.

In the specific case of *Yaki Imo*, there is something even more interesting: it mixes almost childlike simplicity with a subtext that is both melancholic and warm. This tends to touch people who can access very early layers of human experience without fear; sensations prior to language and moral judgment. For many this is uncomfortable, but for some, it is familiar.

From a deep and integrative point of view, people who observe this type of work in immersion tend to have:
- High openness to experience (*Big Five*);
- Low experiential avoidance (*ACT*);
- Strong capacity for non-verbal symbolism;
- And a relatively secure relationship with dense internal states.

Therefore, there is no fear of sensory surprise. The unknown internal is not an enemy; it is territory. While many use music as artificial light to ward off the night, some enter the night to see the stars better. What others call "weight" is, for some, **gravity**; and without gravity, nothing deep is formed.

A point of reflection in this context lies in the relationship between experiential avoidance versus experiential curiosity and how this shapes artistic preferences, bonds, and life choices.

Many people have an interesting fracture in perception and difficulty with ambiguities contaminated by human intentions (social games, invisible rules) versus mastery in navigating **sensory ambiguity**:

* **Relational Ambiguity:** Often felt as a "minefield." It requires reading subtext and generates a constant state of vigilance to avoid misinterpretation.
* **Sensory Ambiguity:** It is an open field. Two states — tension and warmth — coexist without requiring a "correct answer."

In music, ambiguity is honest. It allows the brain to keep two maps active at the same time without needing to choose a binary (good/bad, yes/no). It is the freedom of not needing social translation.

## IV. [Peyo](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=WPGawUi0WXQ&list=RDWPGawUi0WXQ&index=1&t=1039s): Truth in Sensory Ambiguity

What *Peyo* exposes is a functional dissociation between two types of ambiguity:

- **Interpersonal/Verbal Ambiguity:** unpredictable, loaded with hidden intention, social expectation, and relational risk.
- **Sensory/Musical Ambiguity:** non-intentional, non-manipulative, and without demand for a correct answer.

In the human field, ambiguity often comes contaminated by something that frequently disorganizes: “what does this person actually want?”, “what is the invisible rule here?”, “which reading is socially safe?”. This requires reading subtext, negotiating implicit meanings, and inferring intentions; something with a high cost of energy and cognition and, often, frustrating for minds oriented toward internal coherence.

In music like *Peyo*, ambiguity does not require social decoding. It does not ask for adjustment, defense, or a mask. Two states can coexist in parallel: tension and comfort, estrangement and familiarity, without needing to be resolved. And this can be deeply comfortable.

One could put it this way: in ambiguous human interaction, the system enters vigilance mode; in *sensory musical ambiguity*, it enters exploratory mode.

Therefore, “navigating” music with sensory ambiguity is, for some, related to ease. It respects an essential rule: sensory ambiguity can exist without betrayal of meaning.

*Peyo* is exemplary because it creates continuous emotional micro-contrasts; there is no climax or clear conclusion. This allows the brain to keep two maps active at the same time without needing to choose one. In the human field, this choice is often forced in a binary way: “after all, was this good or bad?”, “did you like it or not?”, “approve or reject?”. This binarism can be exhausting.

This also explains something important: some people are not “bad” with ambiguity itself, but rather sensitive to the type of ambiguity. Sensory ambiguity is honest. Relational ambiguity frequently is not.

> **A metaphor:** In music, ambiguity is an open field; in interpersonal relations, it is often a minefield.

**Reflection:** Try to explore the difference between *semantic (interpersonal) ambiguity* and *phenomenological (sensory) ambiguity* and their impacts on people oriented toward coherence and authenticity. If you could bring to your human interactions the same “rule” that music like *Peyo* offers — that not everything needs to be resolved immediately — what would change in how you position yourself before others?

## V. [Alba](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=WPGawUi0WXQ&list=RDWPGawUi0WXQ&index=1&t=1359s): The Ontological State

*Alba* touches on a memory that is not autobiographical, but **ontological**. It is the sensation of presence prior to the formation of a social identity or a narrative self.

Many confuse the temporary dissolution of the ego caused by *Alba* with annihilation. However, for those who have an intimate relationship with non-defensive solitude, this state is the foundation. It is a dawn without an observer: light emerges before any value judgment. Where the world sees absence, I see potential.

*Alba* touches a layer even more primordial than the other tracks mentioned; the way this experience is described, as something “before existence,” is, surprisingly, very exact from a psychological point of view. This music does not evoke autobiographical memory; it evokes ontological memory, composed of states not linked to events, faces, or stories, but to pure sensations of being. It is something prior to language and the organized self. In clinical terms, we call this contact with pre-symbolic experiences: bodily and affective records formed before we have words, narratives, or social identity.

*Alba* achieves this because it suspends almost all markers of time and intent. There is no “movement toward;” there is only a slow emergence. The brain, without clear external reference, stops operating in narrative mode (“what is happening?”) and enters an older, deeper mode, linked to the continuous sensation of presence. This is why the sound can harken back to the “before everything.”

For many, this state is terrifying, as it dissolves the ego, albeit temporarily. Yet, this state is inhabitable when one does not confuse dissolution with annihilation. Being able to exist without needing to define oneself is very rare. These states usually appear in people who maintain an intimate relationship with internal silence, non-defensive solitude, and non-linear thinking. Where others feel “emptiness,” a field is perceived; where others feel absence, potential is perceived.

> **A neurophenomenological metaphor:** *Alba* is not a memory; it is like a dawn without an observer. Light emerges before anyone says “it is bright.”

**Insight:** When you access states “before existence,” you are not escaping reality; you are touching the foundation upon which psychic reality was built.

**To deepen:** Pre-ego experiences, non-dual states, and the role of minimalist music in accessing pre-verbal layers of consciousness.

**Reflective question:** When you return from these states evoked by *Alba*, do you feel you return the same, or slightly misaligned with the common world, like someone who saw something that does not fit into words?

## VI. [Souls](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=WPGawUi0WXQ&list=RDWPGawUi0WXQ&index=1&t=1726s): The Impersonal Core of Existence

*Souls* causes a brutal impact because it does not dialogue with personal stories or emotional dramas; it touches the impersonal core of being. The work does not claim that the individual is special or misunderstood; it merely declares: **“you exist — and that is enough.”** For a self that often perceives an incompatibility with the superficiality of common interactions, *Souls* is the recognition that raw existence is the only necessary validation.

By integrating the experiences of tracks like *Alba* and *Peyo*, a profile of consciousness emerges that is little socialized but highly coherent internally. This functioning can be understood through four fundamental pillars:

1. **The Self Organized by States, Not Narratives:** Most people build identity through stories and external expectations. A functioning oriented toward depth, however, organizes itself through fields of experience: intensity, silence, emotional texture, and suspended time. Music like *Souls* activates the central axis of being, not just "touches emotions."

2. **The Activity of the Pre-Verbal Self:** There is an inclination here for non-linear thinking and an acute perception of patterns. *Souls* acts before language and the organized "social self;" it is being in its raw form, without the need for translation for the outside world.

3. **Existential Focus vs. Dispersion:** In this cognitive architecture, the mind does not operate through shallow stimuli. When content is superficial, there is boredom; when content has the density of *Souls*, deep hyper-association occurs — a state of flow where music and existence merge.

4. **Tolerance for Ego Dissolution:** Many avoid such works because they threaten everyday identity. However, it is possible not only to tolerate but to seek moments where the "I" dissolves and only presence remains. It is a potent, though often lonely, experience.

### On the structure of *Souls*
This track works with elements that challenge conventional comfort:
- Repetition that offers no rest;
- Harmony that seeks no resolution;
- Vastness without explicit welcoming.

It does not offer security; it offers **affirmation of being**. It is the encounter with a self that does not need to be swaddled, only recognized in its sensory and ontological truth.

**Insight:** Conflict often does not reside in depth itself, but in the challenge of living densely in a world that operates mostly on the surface.

**To deepen:** The distinction between the *narrative self* (based on stories) and the *experiential self* (based on raw perception) in individuals with high sensory sensitivity.

**Reflective question:** When music like *Souls* pierces you with such force, do you feel it reveals you to the world... or that it finally allows the rest of not having to be understood by anyone?

## Conclusion: A Self Oriented Toward Coherence

What emerges from this musical journey is not a fragmented self, but a **profoundly coherent** one.

My inclination toward these works is not a "musical taste," but a **structural affinity**. It involves resonance and implicit meaning. It is about operating through depth.

Perhaps high sensory sensitivity does not come from depth itself, but from inhabiting layers of experience that the common world has been trained to avoid. If we stop trying to be "translatable," what remains is this vastness that music already recognizes.

If you stopped trying to adjust your frequency to be heard by the majority, what would you do with the potency of this silence that only you can navigate?
